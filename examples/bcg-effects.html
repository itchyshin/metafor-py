From counts to log risk ratios

Question: How do the reported tuberculosis case counts translate into study effects?

Each row has cases and non-cases for vaccinated and control groups. escalc_rr computes the log risk ratio and its sampling variance. Negative log risk ratios correspond to lower observed risk in the vaccinated group.

The fixture is derived from dat.bcg. Its source, terms, and pinned R reference are recorded in reference/v1/PROVENANCE.md. The page uses original prose and an original plot.

Run the native Python example from the repository root:

python examples/bcg.py --case=effects

The script reads the raw counts from the versioned fixture, computes every study effect, and writes the results for comparison with R and Julia. The Open the local comparison page reports differences by output and tolerance.

Interpretation

The pooled estimates summarize the study effects. They do not adjust for the latitude moderator; that question is handled in meta-regression.

Differences from R metafor

The Python implementation supports the log risk ratio and the checked zero-cell correction options listed in COMPATIBILITY.md. Other escalc() measures are unsupported.
