Dependent effects and supplied covariance

When several estimates share studies or outcomes, their sampling errors may be correlated. The dependent-effects functions accept a supplied sampling covariance matrix V, keeping that known sampling covariance separate from estimated study-level variation.

  • fit_meta_cov(yi, V, X=None, components=None, method="REML", design_columns=None) fits GLS or REML with V and optional supplied positive-semidefinite scalar kernels.

  • fit_meta_nested(yi, V, groups, X=None, group_names=None, method="REML", design_columns=None) builds independent nested random-intercept kernels from grouping vectors. Inner labels should include their parent labels, such as (study, effect).

  • fit_meta_un(yi, V, study, outcome, outcome_levels, method="REML") fits a two-outcome model with an unstructured positive-semidefinite study covariance.

  • fit_meta_cov_formula(data, formula, V, id=None, components=None, method="REML") uses the optional pandas/Patsy table adapter and preserves retained row IDs while masking both axes of V.

For example, the matrix-first form is:

from metafor import fit_meta_cov

fit = fit_meta_cov(yi, V, X=X, method="REML")

Current checks cover correlated GLS, a nested two-component fixture, and a synthetic two-outcome fixture. This is partial coverage of rma.mv(): at most eight independent scalar components are accepted; three or more outcomes, AR/CAR and spatial structures, estimated sampling correlations, small-sample inference, profile intervals, and the full diagnostic suite are outside the current scope. Warning wording and error behavior may differ from R.

Start with the dependent-effects article, then inspect the comparison guide for fixture quantities and tolerances.
